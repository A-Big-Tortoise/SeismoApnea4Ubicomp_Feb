# REF-002: AASM Scoring Manual Version 3 (2023 Update)

**Source:** American Academy of Sleep Medicine. "The AASM Manual for the Scoring of Sleep and Associated Events, Version 3." 2023.

**URL (summary of changes):** https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SummaryofChanges_Document_3-1.pdf

**Editorial:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11063699/

**Type:** Clinical guideline update

---

## Key Updates in Version 3

### Hypopnea Scoring
- The **4% desaturation rule** was downgraded from ACCEPTABLE to **OPTIONAL**
- The **recommended** criterion remains: >=30% amplitude drop for >=10 seconds with >=3% desaturation or arousal
- All AASM-accredited sleep facilities were required to implement Version 3 by December 31, 2023

### Reporting Changes
- Reporting the **central apnea index (CAI)** is now OPTIONAL
- Reporting **time below specified oxygen saturation thresholds** is now RECOMMENDED
- New RECOMMENDED rules for scoring cardiac events (2nd/3rd degree AV block, pacemaker rhythm)

### Clarification on Hypopnea Criteria (AASM 2023 Statement)
From the AASM clarification:
- The recommended hypopnea definition uses a **>=30% drop** in nasal pressure signal excursion for **>=10 seconds** associated with **>=3% oxygen desaturation or an arousal**
- The alternative/optional definition uses **>=4% oxygen desaturation** (without arousal criterion)

---

## Relevance to BCG-based Apnea Detection

The convergence toward the 3% desaturation/arousal criterion means:
1. The field is moving toward **more sensitive** detection of respiratory events
2. For BCG-based detection without SpO2, the amplitude-based criteria (>=90% drop for apnea, >=30% drop for hypopnea) remain the primary detectable features
3. The 10-second minimum duration rule is unchanged and universally applied
4. Since we cannot detect arousals from BCG alone, our detection will inherently be conservative (missing hypopneas that are scored only by arousal criterion)
